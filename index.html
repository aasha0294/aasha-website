Agent or portal

The portal is cheaper right up until something goes wrong.

A booking site is a shop counter. It sells you the ticket and the relationship ends there. That is fine on a good day. Most days are good days.

When this happens A booking portal Aasha Tours
Your 6am flight is cancelled the night before
A chatbot, a helpline queue, and a refund in 7–10 working days.
You call us. We hold the ticket stock, so we rebook you while you are still on the phone.
You need a visa
Not offered. You are on your own with the consulate's checklist.
We build the file, book the appointment and track it. Most rejections are paperwork, and paperwork is what we do.
Eleven of you are travelling, on three different budgets
Eleven separate bookings, and you are the one reconciling them.
One booking, one bill, one person holding the whole thing together.
The company needs a GST invoice for input credit
A generic invoice, often from a gateway in another state.
A proper invoice from a GST-registered agent in Udaipur, made out to your company.
You want to change a date after booking
Change fees, a form, and whatever the portal chooses to pass on.
We handle it with the airline directly. You send one message.
Something goes wrong at 11pm in a foreign country
An international helpline, and a time zone that is not yours.
A number in Udaipur that a person you have met answers.

To be straight with you: for a simple one-way domestic hop booked three weeks out, a portal is quick and the fare is usually fine. Use one. It is everything else — visas, groups, international routings, anything with a date that might move, anything a company has to account for — where having an agent stops being a preference and starts being the difference.
